Texting Back but Your Partner Has Depression 💬🕳️

So here's the situation.

You're texting someone you care about. A partner. Maybe a best friend. And they just… disappear.

BODY

Section 1: The text isn't the task. The task is being a person.

Here's the thing nobody tells you.

When you text someone with depression, you're not asking them to reply. You're asking them to find the energy to exist for a second.

[B-ROLL]

Think about it like this. Imagine your phone battery is at 2%. And every single task — brushing your teeth, getting out of bed, eating — drains it.

Now a text comes in. "Hey, how are you?"

To answer honestly costs 10%. To lie and say "I'm fine" costs 5%. And they don't have it.

[B-ROLL]

So they don't ignore you. They just… can't. The message sits there like a weight they keep meaning to lift.

And every hour it sits, the guilt gets heavier. Which makes replying even harder.

[B-ROLL]

So here's the shift. Their silence isn't "I don't care about you." It's "I don't have the battery to show you I do."

Which brings me to the thing most of us get completely wrong.

Section 2: "Why didn't you just reply?" makes it worse.

I know this feels fair to ask. It really does.

But to someone with depression, "Why didn't you just reply?" doesn't sound like a question. It sounds like proof.

[B-ROLL]

Proof that they failed. Again. Proof that they're a bad partner, a bad friend, a burden.

Here's the analogy. Imagine someone is drowning. And you swim up and yell, "Why didn't you just swim?"

[B-ROLL]

That's what it feels like. The whole reason they went silent is the thing you're now asking them to explain — using energy they already don't have.

So the "why" question digs the hole deeper.

[B-ROLL]

Instead of "Why didn't you reply," try "No pressure to respond — I just wanted you to know I'm thinking of you."

See the difference? One demands energy. The other hands it over.

Now, let's talk about what actually pulls someone out.

Section 3: Lower the bar until it's on the floor.

The biggest kindness you can offer is making the reply ridiculously easy.

When someone's drowning in silence, don't ask open questions. Those require too much. Give them a door they can walk through without thinking.

[B-ROLL]

Instead of "How are you feeling?" — which is a whole essay — try sending a single emoji they can react to.

"Just send me a 👍 if you're alive and a 🫠 if today's rough. That's all I need."

[B-ROLL]

Now they didn't have to form sentences. They didn't have to perform being okay. They just had to tap once.

And here's the magic. That one tap breaks the paralysis. Sometimes the hardest part is the first word. You just gave them a way to say it without saying it.

[B-ROLL]

Lower the bar. Then lower it again. You're not being babyish. You're being a lifeline.

Section 4: Their quiet is not your emergency to fix.

Okay, this one's for you.

Because loving someone with depression can quietly destroy you if you let their silence become your whole world.

[B-ROLL]

Here's the trap. You start reading every "Read" receipt like a lie detector. You replay the last thing you said. You spiral.

And now there are two people drowning.

[B-ROLL]

You can't pour from an empty cup. So you send your kind, low-pressure message — and then you go live your life.

[B-ROLL]

Trust that your message landed. Trust that caring doesn't mean watching the screen. Their recovery isn't a text you can send hard enough.

[B-ROLL]

Loving them well includes not sinking with them.

Which leads to the last thing — the part most people miss entirely.

Section 5: When they come back, don't make them pay for the silence.

This is the moment that decides everything.

They finally reply. Maybe it's hours later. Maybe days. And they're bracing. Waiting for the punishment.

[B-ROLL]

If you hit them with "Oh, NOW you text me?" — you just taught them that coming back is dangerous. So next time? They'll stay gone longer.

[B-ROLL]

But if you say, "Hey, I'm really glad to hear from you. No apology needed." — you taught them something else entirely.

That it's safe to resurface. That you're not keeping score.

[B-ROLL]

Here's the analogy. Imagine coming up for air after holding your breath way too long — and instead of judgment, there's just relief that you made it.

That's what you want to be. The air. Not another reason to go back under.

[B-ROLL]

That one response can be the reason they reach out faster next time.
