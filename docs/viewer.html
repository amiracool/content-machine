# 5 Newly Identified Memory Glitches That Point To Adult ADHD

HOOK (0–30 seconds)

You walk into the kitchen. And you have no idea why you're there.

You stand there. Blinking. Waiting for your brain to send the memo.

If that just made you laugh in that painful "wait, is that not normal?" way… stay with me.

Because researchers have started mapping specific memory glitches that show up in adult ADHD. Not the dramatic kind. The quiet, everyday kind you've been blaming on being "scatterbrained" your whole life.

[B-ROLL]

These five are easy to miss. And by the end, you might finally understand why your brain does the thing it does.

SETUP (30–90 seconds)

Here's the situation.

You're smart. People tell you that. You can hold a whole conversation, crush a project under deadline, remember the plot of a show you watched eight years ago.

But you can't remember to send the email you literally just thought about ten seconds ago.

And it makes you feel broken. Lazy. Like everyone else got an instruction manual you never received.

[B-ROLL]

Here's the thing nobody told you. ADHD isn't just about attention. A huge piece of it lives in your working memory. That's the mental sticky note your brain uses to hold information for a few seconds while you do something with it.

For a lot of adults, that sticky note has no glue.

In the next few minutes, I'm going to walk you through five specific memory glitches that experts now connect to adult ADHD. Not so you can diagnose yourself. But so you can stop feeling like the problem is *you*.

Let's get into it.

BODY

Section 1: The "Why Am I Here" Blank

The first one is losing your intention the second you change rooms.

You think "I need my charger." You stand up. You walk ten feet. And the thought is just… gone. Erased. Like it was never there.

Scientists actually have a name for this. It's called the "doorway effect." Walking through a doorway acts like a mental reset button. For most people, it's a mild glitch. For ADHD brains, it happens constantly and hits harder.

[B-ROLL]

Think of your working memory like a browser tab with a tiny bit of RAM. The second something new loads on screen, the old tab crashes.

It's not that you don't care. The intention was real. Your brain just couldn't hold it long enough to finish the trip.

And here's the kicker. This one rarely gets talked about because it feels too small to mention. But add it up across a whole day and it's exhausting.

Which brings me to the next one.

Section 2: The "Mid-Sentence Vanish"

Number two. You're talking. You're mid-sentence. And the word you need just… disappears.

You know it. It's right there. You can feel the shape of it. But your brain refuses to hand it over.

So you do the thing. "It's like a… the thing… you know, the whatchamacallit."

[B-ROLL]

Researchers link this to something called retrieval failure. In ADHD brains, the information is stored just fine. The filing system is organized chaos. So pulling the right file on demand is where it breaks down.

It's like having a library with every book you need. But the lights are off and someone shuffled all the shelves.

And the worst part? It usually happens when you're excited or under pressure. The exact moments you most want to sound put together.

If you've ever lost your own point in the middle of making it, you know this one personally.

Let's keep going.

Section 3: The "I Definitely Did Not Hear That"

Third glitch. Information goes in one ear and leaves before it registers.

Someone gives you directions. You're nodding. You look like you're listening. You *are* listening. And three seconds later you could not repeat a single word they said.

[B-ROLL]

This is auditory working memory, and it's a big one in adult ADHD. Spoken information is fragile. It doesn't stick to the page the way text does. So verbal instructions slide right off.

Imagine trying to catch water with your bare hands. You're trying. You're really trying. But most of it just runs through your fingers.

This is why you probably prefer things written down. Or why you ask people to text you instead of call. That's not you being difficult. That's your brain building a workaround for a real glitch.

And once you know that, you can stop apologizing for needing instructions twice.

Next one is sneaky.

Section 4: The "Time Collapse"

Number four. You genuinely cannot remember when things happened.

Was that conversation yesterday? Last week? Was that a dream? You remember the event perfectly. But where it sits in time is a total blur.

[B-ROLL]

Experts call this a problem with time-stamping memories. ADHD brains often store the *what* but drop the *when*. So your past feels like a pile of photos with no dates on the back.

This is also why "I'll do it later" is dangerous for us. Later has no real location in time. It's just a fog where tasks go to die.

Think of a normal memory like a photo filed in the right month. An ADHD memory is more like a photo that fell behind the couch. Still there. Just impossible to place.

If you've ever argued with someone about *when* something happened and you both swore you were right — this might be why.

One more. And this is the one people feel the most.

Section 5: The "Prospective Memory Fail"

The fifth glitch is forgetting to do the thing in the future that you 100% meant to do.

This is called prospective memory. Remembering to remember. And it's one of the most consistent findings in adult ADHD research.

It's not forgetting the past. It's forgetting the plan. Taking your meds. Replying to that one text. Grabbing the thing from the car. You *know* you need to. You even set the intention. And the moment to act just slides by without a single alarm going off in your head.

[B-ROLL]

Here's the analogy that stuck with me. Most people have a built-in alarm that fires when the right moment comes. ADHD brains have the alarm. It just isn't plugged in.

That's why you probably live and die by reminders, sticky notes, and phone alerts. If it's not external, it doesn't exist.

And this is the glitch that makes people feel the worst. Because from the outside, it looks like you don't care. You didn't follow through, so you must not have meant it.

But you did mean it. You meant it with your whole heart. Your brain just couldn't carry the plan to the finish line.

That's not a character flaw. That's a wiring difference.

[B-ROLL]

If even two or three of these sounded like your daily life, it might be worth having a real conversation with a professional. Not to label yourself. To finally get the right tools instead of just trying harder with the wrong ones.

None of these glitches mean you're lazy, broken, or not smart enough. They mean your brain holds information differently. And once you understand *how*, you can build a life that works *with* it instead of fighting it every single day.
