# I May Not Finish a Single Task Today, But I Started 14 of Them With Genuine Enthusiasm

HOOK (0–30 seconds)

I may not finish a single task today.

But I started 14 of them. With *genuine* enthusiasm.

[B-ROLL]

Right now there is a load of laundry in my washer that has been "washed" four separate times. Because I keep forgetting to move it. So I keep re-running it.

That's not laziness. That's not a character flaw.

That's an ADHD brain doing exactly what ADHD brains do.

And by the end of this video, you're going to understand *why* — and more importantly, how to actually finish some of those 14 things without hating yourself in the process.

[B-ROLL]

Stick with me. This one hits close to home.

SETUP (30–90 seconds)

Here's the situation you're probably in.

You wake up with a plan. A *good* plan. Today you're going to answer those emails, clean the kitchen, start that project you've been putting off.

[B-ROLL]

And you DO start. All of it. With real excitement.

You open the email. You get the cleaning spray. You pull up the project doc.

And then somewhere between task one and task fourteen... nothing gets finished.

[B-ROLL]

Then comes the part that actually hurts. The voice in your head that says *"What is wrong with you? Why can't you just finish anything?"*

Here's what I want you to know. You are not broken. You are not lazy. Your brain is running a completely different operating system than most productivity advice was written for.

So here's my promise. In the next few minutes, I'm going to show you exactly why your brain does this — and give you three real strategies that work *with* your wiring instead of against it.

Let's get into it.

BODY

Section 1: Your brain runs on interest, not importance

Here's the thing nobody tells you. A neurotypical brain can do a task because it *should* be done. An ADHD brain does a task when it's interesting, urgent, novel, or challenging.

[B-ROLL]

Doctors call this an "interest-based nervous system." I call it the reason I've reorganized my entire bookshelf but haven't opened a bill in three weeks.

Think of it like a car that only runs on premium fuel. You can pour regular gas in all day long. It just won't move. Your brain isn't refusing to work. It's waiting for the right fuel.

[B-ROLL]

So when you start 14 things? Every single one felt genuinely interesting *at the moment you started it.* That enthusiasm was real. The follow-through vanished the second the novelty wore off.

That's not a willpower problem. That's a fuel problem.

And once you get that, you can start using it on purpose. Which brings me to the good part.

Section 2: Finish ONE thing before you're allowed to start another

Okay, strategy time.

The reason you have 14 open loops is because starting feels amazing and finishing feels like nothing. The dopamine hit is at the *beginning.*

[B-ROLL]

So we're going to hijack that. Here's the rule: you have to *close one loop before you open a new one.*

Not all 14. Just one. Pick the closest-to-done task and finish it first.

[B-ROLL]

That laundry I told you about? The trick that finally worked was making the *finish* the interesting part. I set a two-minute timer and turned it into a race against myself. Suddenly it had urgency. Suddenly my brain cared.

You're not forcing yourself to be boring. You're making the finish line the fun part.

Try it today with literally one thing. The half-empty dishwasher. The one reply. Watch how different closing a loop feels.

Section 3: Body-double your way through the boring stuff

Here's a strategy that sounds fake but works like magic.

[B-ROLL]

It's called body doubling. You do a boring task while another human is simply *present* — in the room, on a video call, even in a coffee shop.

Why does this work? Because having someone there adds a tiny bit of accountability and urgency. Remember — urgency is fuel. A body double quietly turns a low-interest task into a slightly-higher-interest one.

[B-ROLL]

Think of it like going to the gym. You *know* how to do a squat at home. But somehow you actually show up when there's a class waiting for you.

Can't find a person? There are literally thousands of "study with me" videos and virtual co-working calls online. Put one on. It counts.

[B-ROLL]

I finished my taxes last year sitting on a video call with a friend who was doing her own taxes. We barely spoke. But we both finished. That's the magic.

Section 4: Make your open loops VISIBLE, not mental

Here's a big one. ADHD brains have "out of sight, out of mind" turned up to eleven.

[B-ROLL]

That laundry I re-washed four times? It disappeared from my universe the moment the lid closed. If I can't see it, my brain genuinely believes it stopped existing.

So stop keeping your tasks in your head. Your head is a terrible filing cabinet. It's a fantastic idea generator and a *horrible* storage unit.

[B-ROLL]

Make everything visible. Sticky notes at eye level. A whiteboard you can't avoid. Clear containers. Leave the vitamins on the counter, not in the cabinet.

The goal isn't to remember more. It's to *need* to remember less.

Externalize the loop, and future-you will actually see it and close it.

Section 5: Talk to yourself the way you'd talk to a friend

Last one. And honestly, it might be the most important.

[B-ROLL]

Every time you don't finish something, there's that voice. *"You're lazy. You're a mess. Everyone else can do this."*

That shame doesn't motivate you. It freezes you. Shame is the number one thing that makes an ADHD brain shut down completely and start scrolling for three hours instead.

[B-ROLL]

So here's your reframe. You did not fail to finish 14 things. You had the *energy and enthusiasm* to start 14 things. That's a brain overflowing with ideas and drive.

Would you tell a friend they're worthless because they got distracted? No. You'd say, "Hey, let's just pick one and knock it out together."

[B-ROLL]

Talk to yourself like that. The kinder you are to your brain, the more it actually cooperates. I'm not being cheesy. Self-compassion is a legitimate ADHD strategy backed by research.

You're not lazy. You're wired differently. And different is workable.
