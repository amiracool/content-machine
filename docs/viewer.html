# I May Lose My Keys Three Times a Day… But My ADHD Just Saved the Entire Project 🤣

HOOK (0–30 seconds)

I lost my keys three times before 9 AM today.

Three times.

I found them in the fridge once. In my shoe another time. And the third time? Still don't know. My neighbor found them in her mailbox.

But here's the part nobody tells you about brains like mine.

That same "disaster" brain? It just caught a mistake that would've cost my whole team six weeks of work. A mistake that four "organized," color-coded, spreadsheet-loving people walked right past.

[B-ROLL]

So before you apologize for your "messy" brain one more time… stay with me. Because I'm about to show you why the thing you hate about your ADHD might be your actual superpower.

SETUP (30–90 seconds)

Let me guess.

You've been called scattered. Flaky. "Not living up to your potential." You've lost the keys, forgotten the birthday, missed the deadline, and walked into a room only to forget why you're there.

[B-ROLL]

And somewhere along the way, you started believing the story. That you're just… bad at things. That everyone else got the instruction manual and you got a broken one.

Here's what I actually want you to walk away with today.

By the end of this video, you're going to understand *why* your brain loses the small stuff — and how that exact same wiring lets you catch the big stuff everyone else misses. You'll be able to spot your own hidden strengths. And you'll stop apologizing for a brain that was never actually broken.

Let me tell you what happened on that project.

BODY

Section 1: Your brain isn't broken — it's tuned to a different channel

Here's the truth. An ADHD brain doesn't have a *storage* problem. It has a *filter* problem.

[B-ROLL]

Think of most brains like a radio locked onto one clear station. Steady. Predictable. Great for remembering where you put your keys.

Your brain? It's scanning every channel at once. Yeah, that's why the keys vanish. But it's *also* why you hear the one weird frequency nobody else is listening to.

On the project, everyone was locked on the plan. The timeline. The tasks. Heads down. Meanwhile my brain was picking up static in the corner — a tiny detail in a client email that didn't match the spec sheet.

[B-ROLL]

Nobody else noticed it. Because their filters were doing their job. Mine was doing what it always does — grabbing everything.

That "everything" is where the gold hides. Let me show you how it actually caught the mistake.

Section 2: Hyperfocus is chaos with a target

People think ADHD means you can't focus. Wrong. It means you can't focus *on demand*. But when something grabs you? You go deeper than anyone in the room.

[B-ROLL]

That mismatched detail in the email? It grabbed me. And I couldn't let it go.

While everyone else moved on to the next task like functional adults, I fell into the hole. I pulled up the original contract. Then three older emails. Then a spec doc from two months ago that everyone forgot existed.

[B-ROLL]

It's like being a dog that catches a scent. The whole world disappears and there's only the trail. Annoying when it's a random Wikipedia rabbit hole at 2 AM. But when that scent is a real problem? Nobody hunts it down like you do.

Forty minutes later, I found it. The client had changed a key requirement in week two — and it never made it into the plan. We'd been building the wrong thing. Quietly. For weeks.

The same brain that can't hold onto keys held onto that loose thread until it unraveled the whole knot.

But hyperfocus alone didn't save it. There was one more thing.

Section 3: You connect dots that don't look connected

This is the part that gets underrated. ADHD brains are pattern machines. You link ideas that "logical" thinkers keep in separate boxes.

[B-ROLL]

When my calm, organized coworkers looked at that email detail, they saw a tiny wording issue. A typo, maybe.

I didn't see a typo. I saw it *connected* to a comment the client made on a call three weeks ago. Which connected to a budget number that always seemed slightly off. Which connected to why one teammate kept getting confused.

[B-ROLL]

Three unrelated things. My brain slammed them together and went, "Oh. These are all the same problem."

That's not magic. That's how ADHD wiring works. When you're scanning every channel, you naturally overhear how two songs on two stations are actually the same melody.

Your "distractibility" is just your brain refusing to keep ideas in tidy, separate boxes. And the messiest boxes are where the breakthroughs live.

So I walked into the room and said the thing.

Section 4: The "chaos" is only chaos until it isn't

Here's what happened next. I raised my hand and said, "I think we've been building the wrong version for three weeks."

[B-ROLL]

Silence. The kind where you feel every eyeball. And for one second, the old story kicked in — *here's the scattered one, causing drama again.*

Then someone pulled up the contract. Checked the date. And the room's energy flipped completely.

[B-ROLL]

Because I was right.

Six weeks of rework, dodged. A client relationship, saved. All because a brain that can't remember where it parked wouldn't let go of a detail that felt wrong.

Here's the reframe I want to burn into your head.

The world calls your brain chaotic because it doesn't run on their rails. But chaos isn't the absence of order. It's just a *different* order — one that catches things straight lines fly right past.

[B-ROLL]

You will still lose your keys. Real talk. That part doesn't magically disappear.

But you get to decide which story you tell about the same brain. The story where you're broken and behind. Or the story where you're the one person in the room built to catch what everyone else misses.

Same brain. Two completely different lives depending on which story you believe.

Section 5: How to actually use this (not just feel good about it)

Feeling seen is nice. But let me hand you something you can use tomorrow.

One. Stop fighting your hyperfocus — aim it. When something grabs you, don't guilt yourself for the rabbit hole. Ask: "Is this a useful trail or a random one?" If it's useful, ride it hard. That's your superpower switching on.

[B-ROLL]

Two. Trust the "something feels off" signal. That itch when a detail doesn't sit right? That's your pattern-brain talking before your logic brain catches up. Don't dismiss it. Investigate it.

[B-ROLL]

Three. Build systems for the *keys*, not the *genius*. Use alarms, one bowl by the door, phone reminders — automate the boring stuff your brain refuses to hold. That frees up your bandwidth for the big catches. You're not lazy for needing the systems. You're smart for saving your energy for what actually matters.

[B-ROLL]

Your brain was never the problem. The *expectation* that it should work like everyone else's — that was the problem.
